I passed my defense on June 19th.

# The Date
On June 19th, or Juneteenth. 

You might be curious or even annoyed about setting my defense on a national holiday, when people are supposed to celebrate and reminded of the end of slavery in this country. 
And it's not a coincidence--it's simply the only day that I can find a two-hour window that works for all my committee members. 

Out of all the work for graduation, working with your committee to decide the defense date is undoubtedly the most difficult.

I find that a few things are helpful during my process.
- poll tools (I was using when2meet)
- start as soon as possible (starting the poll five months before your planned date is not crazy; all professors would understand)
- always be humble and polite but also brave when communicating with professors (I had to replace one of my original committee members as they didn't have a friendly summer schedule to me, I struggled so much to carefully ask him but it turned out they were totally fine with it)


# The Result
I passed.

People who has looked at my publication record all told me that it's almost impossible for me to fail the defense so no need to worry.
After all, I did have a few pubications on IEEE transactions, and usually two is the standard number that indicates that you may start preparing for your graduation.

I am sure people told me this out of good wills; and I could have believed it and be chill and not as intense to prepare. 
I didn't; I couldn't.
Because I was told exactly the same thing for my qualifying exam at my first year. 
It was simply just selecting three papers, read them, present a report. 
And everybody I consulted told me that nobody ever failed such exams ever. 
So I should also pass, right? Statistically.

I failed my qualifying exam; I used a figure in my slides that I wasn't fully familiar with. Luckily the school allowed me to retake this exam.

Once bitten by the snake, one would be afraid of a rope for the next decade. It's a old Chinese saying, and it is what I felt during my whole PhD. 

Nothing is guaranteed. Nobody failed before you doesn't mean that you would neither. 

# The Aftermath
It was a small event--a 20-person classroom only half full, and ~20 more online. Understandable as it's a national holiday.
